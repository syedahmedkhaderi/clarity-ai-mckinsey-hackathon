    Is it one student or the whole class

    Markwise separates a learner's problem from a teaching problem using one rule, and only one mistake on this test crosses it.

    Adds numerators and denominators separately Whole-class problem

    The rule

    When 40% or more of the class make the same mistake, it is treated as a teaching gap and goes on the plan as one group re-teach. Below that, each learner is followed up on their own.

    What happened here

    5 of 12 students made this mistake, which is 42% of the class. On Q4 they each turned 1/3 + 1/4 into 2/7, adding the tops together and the bottoms together. Four of the five did the same thing again on Q8.

    The five students

    Nomsa D., Fatima S., Kwame A., Amira K. and Ibrahim N.

    Why it matters

    If one more student is found not to have this mistake, the share falls to 4 of 12, which is 33%, and the group re-teach is withdrawn from the plan. Three answers on Q4 are waiting for your call, so this can still move.

    Every other mistake on this test was made by three students or fewer. Those are handled individually, and the ones that fit the budget are on the plan.

    Do first: the whole-class re-teach

    One session for the five students who share the mistake, and anyone else who wants to sit in.

    Group re-teach: adds numerators and denominators separately 30 min, priority 95 of 100

    Who

    Nomsa D., Fatima S., Kwame A., Amira K. and Ibrahim N.

    Why

    5 of 12 learners (42%) show this. Above the 40% threshold this is one teaching gap, not 5 separate learner problems.

    What to say

    Cut two paper strips into different numbers of equal pieces and try to add the pieces. The pieces must be the same size before you can count them together.

    Then check

    Ask each of the five to add 1/3 and 1/4 again on paper before the session ends. If they reach 7/12 with the strips in front of them, the next test will show whether it holds without them.
